Sundered is a system that uses many 6-sided dice (d6s) to decide the success or failure of actions in the form of a Check. Unlike many systems, you do not add up your dice, instead counting Hits.

Dice Pools

When making a Check, you roll a number of d6s called your Dice Pool. Most Dice Pools use a combination of an Attribute and a Skill, though other dice pools can exist.

Example: If a Check calls for PHY + Athletics, you would add up your Physique Attribute and Athletics Skill and roll that many d6s.

Modifying a Dice Pool

Sometimes factors can increase or decrease a Dice Pool by adding or subtracting dice. Always apply all modifiers to a Dice Pool before rolling the dice. A Dice Pool can never be reduced to less than 0 dice.

Hits

When rolling a Dice Pool, every 5 or 6 rolled is considered a Hit. Hits are counted in order to decide the magnitude of success.

Example: You roll a Dice Pool of seven dice, getting 1,2,2,3,5,5, and 6. Three dice rolled a 5 or 6, meaning you scored 3 Hits.

Checks

Checks are the primary way success and failure is dictated in Sundered. To make a Check, you roll the Dice Pool for the Check and compare your scored Hits against the Check's Threshold. If you score Hits equal to our greater than the Threshold, you succeed. Additional hits beyond the Threshold may improve the results when applicable. Consult the table below to get an idea what different Thresholds mean.

Threshold Difficulty Examples
1 Routine Balancing on a wide log, finding a small item in your backpack, hiking for a couple hours
2 Standard Climbing a stone wall, navigating a city unfamiliar to you,
3 Difficult Keeping hold of a red hot iron, breaking down a wooden door, steering a ship in stormy weather
4 Formidable
5+ Extraordinary

Example: You want to break down a locked door. The GM calls on you to make a PHY + Athletics (3) Check. You add up the proper Attribute and Skills to make your Dice Pool, apply any modifiers, roll your dice and count your Hits. You rolled exactly 3 Hits! The door comes down without too much hassle.

Checks should only be called when failure is possible and meaningful. Routine actions done under normal circumstances should not require a Check.

Limits

Most Checks have a maximum number of Hits you can score, called a Limit. You apply any applicable Limits after the Check has been rolled. The most common Limits are Physical and Mental Limits, derived from a character's Attributes.

Example: You are making a PHY + Athletics (3) Check. You have a Physical Limit of 4, so no matter how many 5 or 6s you roll, you cannot score more than 4 Hits.

If multiple limits would be applied to a Check, select the lowest.

Mishaps

When a Check is failed and at least half of the dice rolled (rounded up) show a 1, a Mishap occurs. A Mishap introduces an additional complication appropriate to the action, such as attracting unwanted attention, damaging equipment or placing oneself in danger. A successful Check never causes a Mishap, regardless of how many 1s are rolled.

Opposed Checks

When one creature's action is directly opposed by another, both creatures make a Check. The creature with more Hits wins. The difference betweens their results is called Net Hits.

Example: You are attacking a wolf. You score 4 Hits on your attack Check, and the wolf scores 2 Hits on its dodge Check. You win the Check with 2 Net Hits.

When the two creatures score the same number of Hits, they tie. Unless otherwise stated, a tie favors the defender or otherwise preserves the current situation.

Extended Checks

Sometimes tasks are too complex or time-consuming to resolve with a single Check. These are resolved as Extended Checks. An Extended Check has a Threshold and an Interval. You make the associated Check once each Interval and add reduce the Threshold by your total. When you accumulated Hits equal or exceeds the Threshold, the task is completed.

Each time you make a Check as part of an Extended Check, you gain a -1 dice cumulative penalty for any subsequent Checks. If your Dice Pool is reduced to 0 before you complete the task, you fail.

Threshold Difficulty Examples
3 Routine Replacing a wagon wheel, searching a tidy office
9 Standard Repairing a damage suit of armor, creating a simple ritual circle
15 Difficult
24 Formidable Creating a new spell, designing a complex seige weapon
40+ Extraordinary Creating a universal theory of magic, navigating a collapsing civilization back into prosperity
Interval Pace Examples
1 Round Fast
1 Minute Quick
30 Minutes Average
1 Hour Long
1 Day Consuming
1 Week Exhaustive
1 Month Herculean

Example: You are repairing a damage suit of chainmail. The GM calls for an ACU + Smithing (9, 1 Hour) Extended Check. Each hour you make the check. Once your accumulate 12 Hits, the repairs are complete, assuming you don't run out of dice.
